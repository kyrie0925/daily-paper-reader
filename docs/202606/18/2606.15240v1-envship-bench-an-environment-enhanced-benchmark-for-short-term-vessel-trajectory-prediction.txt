## Abstract 

Vessel trajectory prediction is important for intelligent shipping, maritime surveillance, and navigation safety. However, existing pub-lic maritime AIS resources are often limited by inconsistent forecast-ing protocols, uneven data quality, and the lack of benchmark-ready contextual annotations, which hinder fair comparison and context-aware modeling. To address this gap, we present EnvShip-Bench, a unified benchmark for short-term vessel trajectory prediction built from large-scale raw AIS data from the Danish Maritime Au-thority (DMA) and NOAA through a common processing pipeline. EnvShip-Bench adopts a standardized forecasting protocol with 10 minutes of observation, 10 minutes of prediction, and 20-second sampling in vessel-centric local metric coordinates. Beyond the large-scale core benchmark, it provides a quality-first compact sub-set for efficient and reproducible experimentation, together with synchronized environmental and nearby-vessel context extensions. As a result, EnvShip-Bench supports trajectory-only, environment-aware, and interaction-aware forecasting under a unified evaluation framework. Extensive benchmark statistics and analysis demon-strate that EnvShip-Bench offers a standardized, extensible, and context-aware foundation for maritime trajectory forecasting re-search.

## 1 Introduction 

Vessel trajectory prediction is a fundamental problem in intelligent shipping, maritime surveillance, traffic analysis, and navigation safety. Accurate short-term forecasting can support route moni-toring, collision-risk assessment, anomaly detection, and opera-tional decision-making in complex maritime environments. With the increasing availability of Automatic Identification System (AIS) data, learning-based vessel forecasting has attracted growing at-tention [ 3, 12 , 14 , 21 , 25 , 33 , 42 ]. However, the field still lacks a widely adopted benchmark that provides standardized forecasting settings, explicit quality control, and benchmark-ready contextual annotations. A key limitation of existing maritime AIS resources is that they are often used with study-specific preprocessing and evaluation protocols. Different works may adopt different observation and prediction horizons, sampling intervals, vessel-type filters, clean-ing rules, interpolation strategies, and coordinate systems, making results difficult to compare fairly across papers [ 5, 12 , 40 – 42 ]. In addition, many public resources remain trajectory-centric and do not provide synchronized environmental or nearby-vessel context aligned with forecasting samples. This makes it difficult to system-atically study context-aware maritime forecasting, even though vessel motion is strongly influenced by coastlines, waterways, port geometry, and surrounding traffic [8, 13, 26, 29]. To address these limitations, we present EnvShip-Bench, a uni-fied benchmark for short-term vessel trajectory prediction built from raw AIS data from the Danish Maritime Authority (DMA) and NOAA. Both sources are processed through a common pipeline and released under the same forecasting protocol: 10 minutes of observation, 10 minutes of prediction, and 20-second temporal res-olution, represented in vessel-centric local metric coordinates. This design enables consistent evaluation across maritime regions and provides a common basis for both in-domain and cross-domain benchmarking. As shown in Fig. 1, EnvShip-Bench covers diverse vessel cat-egories, with cargo and tanker vessels forming the major share, while the motion statistics span a broad range of realistic operating speeds and historical displacements. EnvShip-Bench is released as a layered benchmark comprising a large-scale core benchmark, a quality-first compact subset, and synchronized context extensions for environmental and nearby-vessel reasoning. Together, these assets support trajectory-only, environment-aware, and interaction-aware forecasting within a unified framework. In summary, our contributions are threefold: 

• We introduce EnvShip-Bench, a unified large-scale benchmark for short-term vessel trajectory prediction, built from heterogeneous

## 2 Related Work 

Maritime AIS Data Resources and Vessel Forecasting Datasets. 

Public maritime AIS resources have long supported vessel behav-ior analysis, maritime traffic mining, and data-driven forecasting. Representative examples include the Danish Maritime Authority (DMA) historical AIS archive [ 6], NOAA/MarineCadastre Acces-sAIS [ 17 , 22 ], the Ushant AIS dataset [ 7], the Brest heterogeneous maritime dataset [ 26 ], and the DTU abnormal-behavior trajec-tory dataset [ 32 ]. However, these resources mainly serve as raw AIS archives, regional processed datasets, or task-specific releases, rather than unified short-term forecasting benchmarks. As shown in Table 1, most existing maritime AIS resources do not provide standardized forecasting samples, fixed splits, or sample-aligned context extensions. This limitation is also reflected in recent sur-veys, which show that vessel prediction studies remain highly het-erogeneous in preprocessing, forecasting horizons, and evaluation settings [12, 33, 42]. Accordingly, existing vessel forecasting methods, including TrA-ISformer [ 21 ], MSTFormer [ 25 ], recurrent predictors [ 3, 14 , 31 ], Seq2Seq-style models [ 36 ], and uncertainty- or interaction-aware approaches [ 13 , 15 , 16 , 19 ], are typically evaluated on independently processed regional datasets under non-unified settings. In contrast, EnvShip-Bench is designed as a benchmark-oriented release with a standardized forecasting protocol, explicit quality control, layered benchmark assets, and synchronized environmental and social-context extensions. 

AIS Reviews and Downstream Maritime Analytics. Beyond trajec-tory forecasting, AIS has become a foundational data source for a wide range of maritime analytics. Existing reviews summarize AIS applications in maritime traffic analysis, safety assessment, anomaly detection, trajectory prediction, and logistics-related de-cision support [ 27 , 34 , 35 , 43 ]. Representative downstream tasks include traffic pattern discovery and route extraction [ 23 ], vessel destination prediction [ 39 ], estimated time of arrival prediction [ 24 ], AIS-based ship classification [ 11 ], and fisheries activity analysis with environmental context [ 10 ]. These studies demonstrate the broad utility of AIS, but they are still commonly developed under task-specific preprocessing and regional data selections, which lim-its comparability and reproducibility. EnvShip-Bench complements this literature by providing a standardized benchmark release for short-term vessel forecasting while remaining extensible to context-aware downstream modeling. 

Context-Aware Trajectory Benchmarks. In pedestrian and autonomous-driving research, trajectory forecasting benchmarks have progres-sively evolved from trajectory-only prediction toward context-aware benchmark design. In crowd forecasting, datasets such as SDD [ 28 ] and methods such as Social-LSTM [ 1 ], Social-STGCNN [ 18 ], and Social-GAN [ 9] highlighted the importance of neighboring-agent interactions and multimodal futures. In autonomous driving, benchmarks such as Argoverse [ 4 ] and nuScenes [ 2], together with models such as Trajectron++ [ 30 ], Scene Transformer [ 20 ], AgentFormer [ 37 ], and HiVT [ 44 ], further established multi-agent context and semantic map structure as core inputs for prediction. Compared with these domains, maritime forecasting remains relatively limited in benchmark standardization and context-aware design. Although some maritime resources include geographic or environmental attributes [ 26 ], and recent vessel-prediction models have begun to incorporate contextual or graph-structured reason-ing [ 8 , 13 , 19 ], most existing settings remain trajectory-centric and do not provide sample-aligned environmental and social context under a unified forecasting protocol. EnvShip-Bench addresses this gap by combining standardization, quality-aware curation, and context-aware extensibility within a single benchmark framework for vessel trajectory prediction.

## 3 Benchmark Construction 

EnvShip-Bench is a layered benchmark for short-term vessel trajec-tory prediction. Instead of releasing a single processed dataset, we organize it into three components: (i) a large-scale core benchmark built from raw AIS archives under a unified forecasting protocol, (ii) a quality-first compact subset for efficient and reproducible experi-mentation, and (iii) synchronized context extensions with environ-mental and nearby-vessel information. Together, these components support trajectory-only, environment-aware, and interaction-aware forecasting. 

## 3.1 Overview and Unified Forecasting Protocol 

All released samples follow the same forecasting setting: 10 minutes of observation, 10 minutes of prediction, and 20-second sampling. Each sample therefore contains 30 observed positions and 30 future positions. For a trajectory window, we denote the observed history as 

X = {( 𝑥 𝑡 , 𝑦 𝑡 )} 𝑇 ℎ

𝑡 =1, 𝑇 ℎ = 30 ,

and the future trajectory as 

Y = {( 𝑥 𝑡 , 𝑦 𝑡 )} 𝑇 ℎ +𝑇 𝑓 

𝑡 =𝑇 ℎ +1, 𝑇 𝑓 = 30 .

The benchmark task is to predict Y from X, optionally with envi-ronmental or social context. To ensure comparability across maritime regions, all samples are represented in vessel-centric local planar coordinates, with the last observed point as the origin and all positions expressed in meters. This avoids scale distortion in raw latitude–longitude space and provides a common evaluation space across DMA and NOAA. Although the two sources differ in geographic coverage, reporting frequency, and metadata completeness, they are processed with the same construction pipeline and forecasting protocol. 

## 3.2 Large-Scale Core Benchmark 

The core benchmark is derived from raw AIS streams through a standardized preprocessing pipeline that converts heterogeneous vessel reports into benchmark-ready forecasting samples with ex-plicit quality control. 

Schema normalization and validity filtering. DMA and NOAA records are first mapped into a common schema containing vessel identifier, UTC timestamp, latitude, longitude, speed over ground (SOG), course over ground (COG), heading, navigation status, and available static metadata such as ship type and vessel dimensions. Timestamps are normalized to UTC, speed is represented in knots, and source-specific sentinel values are converted to missing values. We then discard records with missing identifiers, timestamps, or positions, remove observations outside valid geographic bounds, reject obviously invalid motion values, and resolve duplicates by keeping the most complete record at each vessel–timestamp pair. 

Physical screening, segmentation, and resampling. To suppress syntactically valid but physically implausible AIS artifacts, we apply conservative global motion filters together with ship-type-aware plausibility checks. Continuous trajectories are then constructed independently for each vessel. A segment boundary is introduced when reports are separated by excessive temporal gaps, implied mo-tion is inconsistent with reported dynamics, or local temporal order remains unreliable after sorting. Because raw AIS streams contain many anchored, moored, or nearly stationary records, the bench-mark focuses on meaningful underway motion. Segments domi-nated by prolonged low-speed behavior, negligible displacement, or anchoring-related status are excluded. Within valid segments, short gaps are interpolated on a unified local planar grid, while longer gaps are not bridged. The resulting segments are resampled onto a global 20-second UTC grid and checked once more for residual geo-metric anomalies. From each valid resampled segment, we generate fixed-length forecasting samples using a sliding-window strategy under the shared 30 → 30 protocol. Each sample is additionally annotated with quality-related statistics, including interpolation ra-tio, displacement measures, and continuity indicators. These labels improve transparency and support stricter downstream benchmark variants. 

## 3.3 Quality-First Compact Subset 

We further derive a quality-first compact subset for controlled ex-periments and reproducible baseline development. Rather than 

> 3

randomly subsampling the core benchmark, this subset is explicitly curated to reduce noise, overlap, and unstable short-term motion. In the current release, it focuses on vessel groups with relatively stable and interpretable behavior, namely cargo/tanker and passen-ger/ferry vessels. Each candidate sample is re-evaluated using finer-grained motion and geometry statistics from its historical and future tra-jectories, including path efficiency, displacement structure, speed variability, turning intensity, step-level irregularity, geometric– directional consistency, and interpolation burden. Samples with un-stable local dynamics, excessive interpolation, weak self-consistency, or fragmented motion are removed. We then compute heuristic 

quality and difficulty scores to prioritize clean yet nontrivial sam-ples, followed by stratified sampling across vessel groups, speed regimes, and motion scales. Since sliding-window generation natu-rally introduces strong overlap, we also cap retained windows per vessel and per segment, and enforce a minimum spacing between selected windows from the same segment. The compact subset should therefore be viewed as a curated derivative benchmark rather than a scaled-down replica of the full release. It preserves the same forecasting protocol while emphasiz-ing sample quality, diversity, and research usability. 

## 3.4 Context Extensions 

To support context-aware maritime forecasting, we augment the compact subset with two synchronized context extensions: envi-ronmental context and target-centric social context. Both preserve the original split membership and prediction target, so context-aware and trajectory-only models can be evaluated under the same setting. 

3.4.1 Environmental Context. The environmental context exten-sion moves beyond line-only shoreline cues toward a mixed rep-resentation that explicitly models land–water structure and local navigability. For each target sample, we recover the absolute geo-graphic anchor at the end of the observation window and construct a target-centered local patch in the vessel coordinate frame. As shown in Fig. 2, the released package includes aligned vector ob-jects, rasterized semantic layers, and lightweight descriptors. The vector layer preserves shoreline, waterfront, breakwater, pier, and related structures. The raster layer encodes land, water, navigable water, natural and man-made boundaries, barrier regions, and signed-distance fields to shoreline and navigable space. The descriptor layer summarizes nearest-shore distance, barrier dis-tance, water ratio, navigable ratio, boundary density, scene type, and environment-quality flags, together with global anchors and local/global transform metadata. This design treats polygon- and occupancy-based semantics as the primary representation, rather than relying only on line fea-tures. To demonstrate benchmark usage, we further construct an environment-aware Social-LSTM baseline with an additional envi-ronment branch and feasibility-aware losses, including a point-level penalty for predictions falling on land or non-navigable regions and a segment-level crossing penalty for trajectories cutting through land or barriers. 

3.4.2 Target-Centric Social Context. The social-context extension provides synchronized nearby-vessel information for each tar-get sample. Rather than reconstructing large scene-centric traffic graphs, the current release adopts a target-centric design: for each sample, we revisit globally referenced resampled trajectories and retrieve neighboring vessels around the end of the observation window within a 5000m fixed radius centered on the target vessel. For each retained neighbor, we export aligned relative history to-gether with descriptors such as relative distance, vessel type, direc-tional attributes, relative velocity components, closest point of ap-proach (CPA), and time to CPA (TCPA). All samples in the compact subset are retained, while interaction-rich cases are marked through dedicated flags. This makes the extension suitable for controlled studies of vessel interaction without sharply reducing dataset size. 

## 4 Benchmark Analysis, Experimental Protocol, and Baselines 

We analyze EnvShip-Bench from three perspectives: comparison with existing maritime AIS resources, statistics and quality char-acteristics of the released assets, and benchmark tasks with initial baselines under the standardized setting. 

## 4.1 Comparison with Existing Maritime AIS Resources 

Table 1 compares EnvShip-Bench with representative public mar-itime AIS resources and processed datasets. Most existing releases are raw AIS archives, processed regional corpora, or task-specific datasets, and therefore do not provide a unified short-term fore-casting protocol, official fixed splits, explicit quality control, or sample-aligned context annotations. EnvShip-Bench differs in three main aspects. First, it is released as a benchmark rather than a raw repository, with explicit pre-processing, filtering, segmentation, and window-generation rules. Second, it adopts a unified forecasting protocol across DMA and NOAA regions, enabling direct comparison across both models and maritime domains. Third, it provides a layered release , includ-ing a large-scale core benchmark, a quality-first compact subset, and synchronized environmental and social-context extensions. EnvShip-Bench is therefore intended not to replace existing AIS repositories, but to transform heterogeneous public maritime data into a standardized benchmark for context-aware vessel trajectory prediction. 

## 4.2 Dataset Statistics and Quality Analysis 

The core release provides standardized forecasting samples at scale for broad evaluation and cross-domain study, while the compact subset emphasizes cleaner and more stable short-term motion for controlled experiments. The context extensions preserve split con-sistency and sample alignment, enabling context-aware benchmark-ing without changing the prediction target. As shown in Fig. 3, sampled anchors cover the main released maritime regions with clear geographic concentration patterns across splits. ## 4.3 Experimental Protocol 

All benchmark tasks use the same train/validation/test split defined by the released assets. Unless otherwise stated, models are trained on the training split, hyperparameters are selected on the validation split, and final results are reported on the test split. Since all samples follow the same 30 → 30 protocol in local metric coordinates, results are directly comparable across models and data domains. For deterministic prediction, we use Average Displacement Error (ADE) and Final Displacement Error (FDE), both measured in meters. For multimodal predictors, minADE and minFDE over a fixed num-ber of generated futures can also be reported. When context-aware 

> 5

models are evaluated, the prediction target and metrics remain un-changed; only the input modality differs. Because DMA and NOAA are processed under the same pipeline, the benchmark also supports both in-domain and cross-domain evaluation. For the initial baseline study, all models are trained for 80 epochs under the deterministic setting. We consider two target-history input configurations: coords , which uses only relative trajectory coordinates, and coords_sog_cog , which augments coordinates with speed over ground and course over ground. The recurrent and Transformer baselines are evaluated as trajectory-only predictors, while Social-LSTM and its environment-aware variant serve as lightweight references for context-augmented modeling. 

## 4.4 Initial Baselines 

EnvShip-Bench supports three progressively richer tasks un-der the same forecasting protocol: trajectory-only forecasting, environment-aware forecasting with aligned geographic context, and target-centric interaction-aware forecasting with synchronized nearby-vessel context. Table 2 reports the initial deterministic baseline results. Under coordinate-only input, simple sequence models remain strong references: Seq2Seq achieves the best ADE at 59.57 m, while TrAISformer yields the best FDE at 130.34 m. GRU is also competitive, with 59.72 m ADE and 133.69 m FDE, indicating that the benchmark is not biased toward a single model family. Kinematic augmentation is not uniformly beneficial. Adding SOG and COG substantially improves TrAISformer, which achieves the best performance under coords_sog_cog with 61.69 m ADE and 125.41 m FDE, and also improves Bi-LSTM. In contrast, Seq2Seq, GRU, Bi-GRU, LSTM, Social-LSTM, and Social-LSTM+Env degrade under the same augmentation, suggesting that auxiliary kinematic attributes help only when the architecture can exploit them effec-tively. Lightweight context modeling yields a consistent but limited gain in the current setup. Social-LSTM+Env improves over Social-LSTM in both input configurations, indicating that environmental cues are useful even in a simple deterministic reference setting. However, these context-augmented variants do not yet surpass the strongest trajectory-only baselines, suggesting that effectively leveraging maritime environmental and interaction information remains an open benchmark challenge. Overall, the baseline results show that EnvShip-Bench is neither trivial nor saturated: performance is not determined solely by model complexity, and the unified protocol reveals meaningful differences across architectures, input formulations, and context usage. The reported baselines provide a practical reference point for future environment-aware, interaction-aware, and multimodal extensions. 

## 4.5 Limitations and Discussion 

EnvShip-Bench still has several limitations. First, the current com-pact subset exhibits a clear long-tail imbalance: approximately 92% of the samples correspond to open-water and weak-interaction conditions, while nearshore, harbor, narrow-channel, and other strongly constrained scenarios are underrepresented. As a result, environmental and social-context inputs are often only weakly in-formative for many samples. ## 5 Conclusion 

We presented EnvShip-Bench, a unified benchmark for short-term vessel trajectory prediction built from DMA and NOAA AIS data under a standardized forecasting protocol. The benchmark includes a large-scale core release, a quality-first compact subset, and syn-chronized environmental and social-context extensions, support-ing trajectory-only, environment-aware, and interaction-aware forecasting within a consistent evaluation framework. We hope EnvShip-Bench will facilitate fair comparison, reproducible exper-imentation, and broader study of context-aware maritime fore-casting. The preprocessing pipeline, raw-data access instructions, and processed benchmark assets are publicly available through the project repository: https://github.com/mark000071/EnvShip-Bench_Large_Dataset_Pipeline_and_datasets.
