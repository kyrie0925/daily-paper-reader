A B S T R A C T 

Coherent Point Drift (CPD) is widely used for rigid point cloud registration because of its soft correspondences and closed-form parameter updates. However, CPD’s target-side marginal constraint forces every observation, including outliers, to receive exactly unit probability mass. This assumption degrades registration accuracy under heavy outliers and partial overlap. Optimal transport (OT) methods can handle missing mass through unbalanced formulations, but require hand-tuned annealing schedules. In this paper, we propose Sinkhorn-CPD , which replaces CPD’s target-side marginal constraint with dual Kullback–Leibler penalties, allowing the algorithm to discard outliers on both sides. The resulting formulation is a fully unbalanced entropic optimal transport problem , which can be efficiently solved by generalized Sinkhorn iterations. Moreover, Sinkhorn-CPD preserves the closed-form Procrustes and variance updates of CPD. In our method, the variance 𝜎 2 plays the role of the entropic regularization parameter, which induces an automatic annealing schedule from diffuse to sharp correspondences without manual temperature tuning. Experiments on synthetic, cross-category, and scan-to-CAD benchmarks show that Sinkhorn-CPD achieves state-of-the-art accuracy, with strong robustness to outliers and partial overlap.

In this work, we prove that CPD’s E-step is equivalent to solving a one-sided entropic OT problem, and the vari-ance 𝜎 2 acts as the entropy temperature. This equivalence reveals two things: (i) the one-sided marginal constraint is the structural cause of CPD’s outlier sensitivity, and (ii) the EM variance update is a data-adaptive entropy annealing schedule. Motivated by this analysis, we replace the one-sided hard marginal with dual-sided Kullback–Leibler (KL) penalties while retaining the CPD-induced Gaussian log-likelihood as the transport cost. Building on this, we propose Sinkhorn-CPD , which for-mulates rigid registration as an unbalanced entropic OT problem solved by generalized Sinkhorn iterations. It rejects outliers on both sides, retains the closed-form Procrustes and variance updates of CPD, and inherits automatic coarse-to-fine annealing through 𝜎 2 without manual temperature tuning. The main contributions are as follows: 

• We prove that CPD’s E-step solves a one-sided en-tropic OT problem, identifying the variance 𝜎 2 as the entropy temperature and the target marginal constraint as the structural cause of outlier sensitivity. 

• We cast rigid registration as an unbalanced entropic OT problem with dual-sided KL relaxations. The pro-posed Sinkhorn-CPD algorithm rejects outliers on both sides via generalized Sinkhorn iterations while maintaining closed-form updates. 

• Experimental results show that Sinkhorn-CPD achieves state-of-the-art performance on synthetic data and real-world tasks, with strong robustness to outliers and partial overlap. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. ICP and Correspondence-Based Methods 

ICP [6] alternates between hard nearest-neighbor cor-respondences and least-squares rigid estimation. Trimmed ICP [7] improves robustness to partial overlap by discarding point pairs with large residuals. Sparse ICP [8] replaces the least-squares objective with an 𝓁 𝑝 (𝑝 ≤1) penalty to down-weight outliers. Zhou et al. [9] combined FPFH fea-tures [10] with a Geman–McClure objective for Fast Global Registration (FGR), avoiding iterative closest-point queries. Yang et al. [11] proposed TEASER++, which uses graph-based correspondence pruning and graduated non-convexity to tolerate extreme outlier ratios with certifiable guarantees. These methods work well given a good initialization or distinctive local features, but their hard-assignment nature limits robustness under noise and low overlap. 

2.2. Probabilistic Registration 

Probabilistic methods replace hard correspondences with soft responsibilities. Granger and Pennec [12] proposed multi-scale EM-ICP, which uses soft assignments within a coarse-to-fine hierarchy. Jian and Vemuri [13] represented both point sets as Gaussian mixtures and minimized their 𝐿 2

distance (GMMReg). Myronenko and Song [1] introduced Coherent Point Drift (CPD), which models source points as isotropic Gaussian centroids with a uniform outlier compo-nent and updates the rigid transform and variance in closed form via EM. Gao and Tedrake [14] proposed FilterReg, which casts the E-step of probabilistic registration as a Gaus-sian filtering problem, achieving speed close to ICP while retaining probabilistic robustness. Hirose [15] reformulated CPD in a Bayesian framework with an acceleration scheme (BCPD). Liu et al. [16] incorporated local surface geometry into CPD to improve feature discrimination (LSG-CPD). Zhao et al. [17] proposed a consistent clustering method for cross-modality registration. 

2.3. Optimal Transport-Based Registration 

Optimal transport (OT) provides a principled framework for comparing and aligning distributions [18, 19]. Cuturi [3] showed that entropic regularization makes OT scalable and differentiable via the Sinkhorn algorithm [20]. Bonneel and Coeurjolly [5] proposed SPOT, which solves partial OT through sliced 1D projections. Shen et al. [21] proposed RobOT, which uses unbalanced OT as a robust matching layer to improve both optimization-based and learning-based registration. Qin et al. [2] formulated registration as partial OT with hard marginal constraints to control the matched fraction (RPOT). Ma et al. [22] augmented the classical discrete OT model with orthogonal and diagonal matrix terms and proposed a relaxed variant for outlier handling. On the theoretical side, Chizat et al. [4] established the scaling algorithm framework for KL-penalized unbalanced OT, and Feydy et al. [23] introduced Sinkhorn divergences that interpolate between OT and MMD, providing positive and convex geometric losses. 

2.4. Learning-Based Approaches 

Deep networks learn feature descriptors or direct pose re-gressors for registration. Aoki et al. [24] combined PointNet features with a Lucas–Kanade update (PointNetLK). Wang and Solomon [25, 26] proposed DCP and PRNet, which learn correspondences and solve weighted Procrustes prob-lems. Yew and Lee [27] combined robust point matching with learned features (RPM-Net). Qin et al. [28] introduced GeoTransformer, which uses geometric attention for coarse-to-fine matching.

5.2. Mechanistic Validation on 2D Data 

We use a 2D example to show the effect of target-side outliers. The source  is a 91-point fish contour. The target  contains 71 inliers (a −60 ◦ rotation and translation 

(−2 , −2) applied to the source) plus 100 Gaussian outliers, creating a 58 .5% outlier ratio. As shown in Fig. 1, CPD is severely affected by outliers, leading to alignment failure. RPOT [2] discards some out-lier mass and achieves near-perfect alignment, but its fixed geometric decay schedule ( 𝜀 ← 0.9 𝜀 ) causes slow conver-gence: even after 100 iterations, it has not fully converged. Sinkhorn-CPD discards outlier mass on both sides through dual KL penalties and converges quickly due to its data-adaptive annealing. 

Vote (influence) visualization. 

To explain the underlying mechanism, we analyze the per-target vote weights that drive the rigid Procrustes update. 

Definition 2 (Vote Weights) . In the M-step, both CPD and Sinkhorn-CPD solve a weighted Procrustes problem. The 

vote of a target point 𝐱 𝑛 is the sum of its column entries in the correspondence matrix: 𝑣 𝑛 = ∑𝑚 𝐏 𝑚𝑛 ∕ ∑𝑚,𝑛 𝐏 𝑚𝑛 for CPD and 𝑣 𝑛 = ∑𝑚 Γ𝑚𝑛 ∕ ∑𝑚,𝑛 Γ𝑚𝑛 for Sinkhorn-CPD. 

The vote weight 𝑣 𝑛 quantifies how much 𝐱 𝑛 influences the Procrustes fit. The target centroid 𝝁 𝑥 = ∑𝑛 𝑣 𝑛 𝐱 𝑛 is the weighted average of the target points. If outliers receive sig-nificant vote weight, they bias the centroid and the resulting poor alignment. Fig. 2 compares the vote distributions of CPD and Sinkhorn-CPD. As shown in the heatmap strip (d), CPD assigns 57 .5% of the total vote weight to outliers, which dominate the transformation. Sinkhorn-CPD suppresses out-lier votes to 0.0% , concentrating the optimization strictly on inlier correspondences.

5.6.3. Variance Annealing Mechanism 

We ablate the 𝜎 2 update mechanism by comparing: (i) Full : standard Sinkhorn-CPD with automatic 𝜎 2 updates; (ii) Fixed-𝜎 2: 𝜎 2 frozen at its initial value (no annealing); (iii) No-𝜎 2: cost matrix uses only squared distances without 

𝜎 2 normalization. Fig. 10(c) reports RE across all four perturbation axes. The full model with automatic annealing consistently out-performs both ablated variants, confirming that variance-driven cost normalization is critical for robust registration. Fig. 5(c) provides per-iteration evidence: Sinkhorn-CPD’s 𝜎 2 tracks the alignment residual from above, while RPOT’s fixed geometric decay ( 𝜀 ← 0.9 𝜀 ) cannot slow down when the residual stays large, and CPD’s 𝜎 2 collapses prematurely. Variance-driven annealing is what makes the difference. 

5.6.4. Effect of 𝜏 𝑦 on Overlap Robustness 

Fig. 10(d) compares the two 𝜏 𝑦 settings under varying overlap. At high overlap ( 𝑜 ≥ 0.7), both achieve sub-degree RE; as overlap decreases, 𝜏 𝑦 =0 .1 degrades more gracefully (RE = 11 .7◦ at 𝑜 =0 .4) than 𝜏 𝑦 =1 .0 (RE = 31 .5◦), because the stronger target-side penalty suppresses non-overlapping mass more effectively. In practice, this is the trade-off when choosing 𝜏 𝑦 : partial-overlap robustness versus convergence basin width (Section 5.3). 

5.7. Computational Cost 

Tables 3 and 5 detail the wall-clock execution times. On the Bunny benchmark ( 𝑁 = 3 ,000 ), Sinkhorn-CPD runs in 4.09 s, matching the efficiency of RPOT (3.96 s) and running faster than CPD (5.49 s). On ModelNet40 ( 𝑁 = 2 ,048 ), it requires 0.53 s. The computational bottleneck is the (𝑀𝑁 )

pairwise distance calculation. Because Sinkhorn-CPD inher-its CPD’s data-driven annealing, it typically converges in 15–30 outer iterations without overshooting. The 𝐿 = 20 

inner Sinkhorn iterations introduce minimal overhead rela-tive to the distance computation and closed-form Procrustes updates. 

## 6. Assumptions and Limitations 

Sinkhorn-CPD inherits several modeling assumptions from CPD that bound its applicability: 

Isotropic, homogeneous noise. A single scalar 𝜎 2 mod-els noise uniformly across all directions and points. Extend-ing to per-point or directional covariances would require replacing the scalar Sinkhorn scaling with a matrix form. 

Known scale. Only rotation and translation are esti-mated; source and target must share the same metric scale. 

Gaussian cost. The squared-distance cost assumes Gaus-sian inlier noise. Dual-KL relaxation provides empirical robustness up to 70% outliers (Section 5.3), but for heavy-tailed inlier noise a robust cost would be more appropriate. 

Local convergence. Sinkhorn-CPD is a local method: convergence is reliable up to 80 ◦ initial rotation ( RE=0 .75 ◦)but fails at 90 ◦ (RE=37 .68 ◦). Under poor initialization, the transport plan can concentrate on a wrong subset, trigger-ing premature 𝜎 2 collapse. A global initializer (RANSAC, TEASER++) resolves this in practice. 

Partial overlap. Under severe partial overlap ( 𝑜 ≤ 0.5), non-overlapping mass biases the weighted centroid despite dual-KL relaxation. Degradation is gradual: RE < 0.84 ◦ for 

𝑜 ≥ 0.6, rising to 11 .66 ◦ at 𝑜 =0 .4.

## 7. Conclusion 

We proposed Sinkhorn-CPD , which formulates rigid registration as an unbalanced entropic OT problem with dual KL penalties on both marginals while retaining CPD’s Gaus-sian log-likelihood cost. The algorithm alternates general-ized Sinkhorn scaling with closed-form Procrustes and vari-ance updates, achieving symmetric outlier rejection without manual temperature schedules or training data. On the Stanford Bunny benchmark, Sinkhorn-CPD with 

𝜏 𝑦 =0 .1 maintains RE < 0.41 ◦ at 70% outlier ratio, where CPD collapses beyond 20% . On ModelNet40 with unseen categories, it achieves 88 .9% registration recall, surpassing GeoTransformer ( 81 .2% ) without any training. Sinkhorn-CPD inherits the (𝑀𝑁 ) complexity and lo-cal convergence basin of CPD, which limits its scalability to very large point sets and its capture range for extreme initial misalignments. We plan to explore scalable kernel 12 approximations ( e.g. , Nyström) to reduce the pairwise dis-tance bottleneck, and to extend the framework to non-rigid registration by incorporating deformation models.

## D. Numerical Stabilization 

Two safeguards are applied in the Sinkhorn-CPD implementa-tion. (i) A floor 𝜎 2 ≥ 10 −8 is enforced after each variance update. Under low-noise, low-outlier configurations the inlier residuals can shrink within a few iterations, and the unconstrained closed-form 

𝜎 2 would otherwise approach 0 and trigger numerical overflow in the Gibbs kernel exp(− 𝐶 ∕(2 𝜎 2)) . (ii) Inside the generalized Sinkhorn iterations, divisions 𝐛 ⊘ (𝐊𝐯 ) and 𝐚 ⊘ (𝐊 ⊤𝐮 ) use a small additive 𝜖 = 10 −12 in the denominator to avoid zero-mass edge cases. Both constants are well below the precision of all reported metrics and do not affect convergence behavior.

The central issue is cost-scale drift . RobOT and RPOT both use the raw squared distance ‖𝐱 −𝑇 (𝐲 )‖2, whose magnitude drops by orders of magnitude as registration progresses. All regulariza-tion coefficients—entropy weight 𝜀 , KL penalty 𝜌 , or partial-mass threshold 𝛽 𝑚 —are calibrated against the initial cost scale. As the cost shrinks, these coefficients become progressively mismatched: in RobOT, the fixed 𝜀 and 𝜌 over-regularize near convergence; in RPOT, the geometric decay 𝜀 ← 0.9 𝜀 is blind to the actual residual, forcing premature sharpening when alignment stalls and unnecessarily slow convergence when alignment is easy. Sinkhorn-CPD resolves this by normalizing the cost with the adaptive 𝜎 2: as residuals shrink, 𝜎 2 shrinks proportionally, so the ratios between cost, entropy, and KL terms remain stable throughout optimization. This is why a single 𝜏 =1 works across all experimental settings without retuning.
